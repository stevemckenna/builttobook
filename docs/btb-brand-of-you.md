# Built To Book: Brand of You (identity layer for all content)

Source: brand-identity exercise from a private training video, worked through for Built To Book (2026-09-29). Private reference only. Do not quote it or name the source in any post.

Status key: **Confirmed** = Steve said it. **Draft** = Claude's assumption, Steve to correct.

Builds on: `btb-niche-profile.md`, `btb-offer-worksheet.md`. Use this sheet as the reference when writing any post, email or script. If new content contradicts it, the story looks inconsistent and tends to flop. When the brand evolves, reference the earlier stance first, then explain the shift.

Principle: tactics attract lower-quality clients; systems attract a step up; philosophy and a world people want to belong to attract the best clients.

---

## 1. Whose brand (Confirmed)

**Steve is the face.** His story, philosophy and voice front the brand. Built To Book is what he sells. Content is written in Steve's voice with the Built To Book visual identity and voice rules (Capable, Direct, Dependable; plain talk; no jargon). Client-facing copy does not use "Grnded" unless Steve asks.

## 2. Philosophy

**Macro (Draft):** Good contractors lose jobs to worse ones with better presentation. Great work should get the credit it deserves online.

**Micro (Draft):** Owners should not be chained to the phone 24/7. A good system answers fast, treats customers well, and gives the owner their evenings back. That is a favor to the customer and to the owner. (Matches the confirmed "doing customers a favor" message.)

## 3. Your world

- **Characters (Confirmed):** Keep family out of content. No family characters, nicknames or names. Steve appears as himself. Do not mention family details in scripts.
- **Language (Draft):** the next job, booked out, answer every call, the truck, the crew, straight talk, good techs. Avoid jargon (funnel, conversion, lead gen, leverage, disruptive, AI-powered).
- **Places (Draft):** Calgary and the trades Steve has worked in.
- **Side quests (Open):** something Steve does alongside the business that people can follow. Not chosen yet.
- **Values (Draft):** Capable, Direct, Dependable, plus craft. Show them in action (a fast reply, an honest quote, a launch on the promised date), not just state them.

## 4. Storylines

Set the origin story at the stage of the audience.

- **Origin story (Confirmed setting):** The scaling years at the garage epoxy flooring brand and the countertop fabricator, when the business was busy and growing and follow-up, presentation and customer response were the pressure points. Speaks to established owners in the same stage. Everything earlier can appear as supporting color. Steve's rule: no specific revenue numbers; get approval on exact wording before using any figure.
- **Vehicle story (Open):** How Steve found the method he now sells (the systems that answered every lead and made the work look as good as it was).
- **Internal story (Open):** The worry, fatigue and doubt during that stage. This answers objections.
- **External story (Open):** The practical fix that worked.
- **Reintroduction post (recommended):** After Steve approves this sheet, write a "here is who I am and how I see it" post and email. Day one of the story.

## 5. Running themes and identity (Draft)

- **Meme or running theme:** Keep it light. Candidate: "answer every call," and the idea of a front desk that never clocks out. A cute persona would clash with the brand; do not force one.
- **Identity (tribe name):** Draft ideas: "booked-out crews" or "Built To Book crews." Video says do not overthink it; any label will feel cringe at first and stops feeling that way with repetition.

## 6. Enemies (Confirmed: practices and types, never named people or companies)

1. Agencies that overpromise and lock you in
2. Template sites that hide great work
3. Chasing every lead by hand
4. Agencies that do not understand the details of running a trades company

Keep the tone about the practice, not personal attacks. Never name competitors. Each enemy maps to a rotating pain (see `btb-offer-worksheet.md`).

## 7. Mechanism (Open)

A name for how it works, made from two ordinary words that are interesting together, plus one short explanation. Nothing chosen. Draft ideas: the Front Desk System, the Booked Loop, the Job-Ready Site. Test a few in content before committing.
