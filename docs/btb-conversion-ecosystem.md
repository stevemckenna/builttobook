# Built To Book: Conversion Ecosystem

Last updated: 2026-09-29. Status: draft plan, in review. Nothing here has been built or sent yet.

Source: three private training sessions (conversion ecosystem parts 1 and 2, and a week-one stats update). Private reference only: no quotes, no name-dropping. Steve's chosen answers are marked **[Steve]**.

Terms note: "funnel" and "ecosystem" are internal words. Never use them in client-facing or public copy.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

One always-on system that turns attention into conversations. Profiles and posts send people to one simple request page. Everyone who requests a mockup sees a confirmation page that makes a clear offer. A short email sequence then follows, with direct messages running alongside it. Anyone who does not buy moves onto the regular email list, where offers go out two to three times a week. It runs whether or not Steve is posting that day.

## 3. The flow

1. **Profile as landing page.** Facebook page and Instagram bio state one clear offer (a free website mockup) and point to one link.
2. **Request page.** Today this is the live homepage with the GHL form. It already has one main action. If data shows the homepage leaks cold traffic (many views, few requests), build a stripped-down single-purpose page with no navigation: benefit headline, short form, "what happens next". Test this after about two weeks, not before.
3. **Confirmation (thank-you) page.** This is the line in the sand: it confirms the mockup request, says what happens next, and offers a 15-minute call with the calendar. Expect few bookings from this page; its job is to make the offer visible and pick up the odd booking.
4. **Email sequence** (section 5), then **regular offer emails** (section 6).
5. **DMs** (section 7) run alongside, pointing people to the same mockup link.

## 4. Lead magnet: the mockup, plus an optional video audit

**The mockup is the offer.** Keep every message about one thing so the owner is never choosing between offers.

**Video audit as a bonus.** Email one asks the owner to reply "got it" for a short personal video (about 3 minutes, screen plus voice) covering their Google Business Profile, their website on mobile, and two or three concrete fixes. Replies signal intent, and the video shows operator insight. Then send the mockup or the next step, and log the deal in the pipeline (stage "Mockup requested").

**Capacity guard.** Every mockup and every video costs time. Set a fit check (established business, real work to show, decision maker), cap builds and videos per day, and only record for people who reply "got it". Keep a checklist so each video takes under 10 minutes.

**Honesty guard.** Audits reflect what Steve actually sees. No invented problems, no fake scores.

## 5. Five-email welcome sequence (GHL)

Send email one within about five minutes of the request. Space the rest tightly for the first few days, then stop.

| # | Purpose | Contents | Call to action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm and ask | Thanks, what happens next with the mockup, ask for reply "got it" for the video audit | Reply "got it" |
| 2 | Cross-platform follow | Link to the latest Facebook or Instagram post or Short so they follow across platforms | Reply "got it" |
| 3 | Who Steve is and what to expect | Short welcome, what he talks about, brief credibility line (has run trade businesses; no figures without approval) | Reply "got it" |
| 4 | Story-based case study | Steve's own experience scaling the epoxy or countertop company: the problem, what he tried, what changed, the result | Reply for details or book a call |
| 5 | Own results and how | Summary of how he grew those businesses and what he built for trades owners from it | Book a call |

Notes:

- **Honest drama.** The training uses "dramatic" case studies. For Built To Book, keep the tension real: true moments from Steve's own scaling years (the missed calls, the slow quote, the weekend spent chasing leads). Do not invent characters, quotes or results. No client case study exists yet; add one only with permission.
- **Own results beat client results.** Steve's own experience is the strongest proof he has right now. Get his approval on wording before any number goes in.
- **After email five:** the line-in-the-sand moment. Offer the entry package directly (Google Ready), then move them onto the regular list.
- The training's paid-call offer does not fit here. Steve's entry offer is a package, not a paid consulting hour.

## 6. Regular offer emails

Two to three a week, story first, one call to action at the end (book a call or reply). Rotate the lead pains, as decided in the offer worksheet: missed calls and slow follow-up; website worse than the work; not showing on Google; owner tied to the phone; hiring. Tie the October Fast-Action Bonus in only while it is real and only quote remaining spots if the count is accurate.

## 7. DMs

Warm and inbound only on Facebook and Instagram at first. Message people who reacted, commented, or replied. Ask a question, then send the mockup link. Starter scripts are in `docs/btb-mvf-map.md`. Cold DMs from a brand-new page risk account restrictions; avoid them for now.

## 8. Tracking (optimize for sales, not requests)

Track weekly, in the GHL pipeline and a simple sheet:

- Page views and mockup request rate, by source (tracked links)
- DMs sent, replies
- "Got it" replies and video audits sent
- Mockups sent, calls booked (email versus confirmation page)
- Email open and click rates per email
- Sales, by package

**Reference numbers from the training (not benchmarks).** In one week, the trainer saw 137 page views, 81 opt-ins (59%), 185 DMs, 25 confirmed replies (13%), five calls booked from emails and none from the thank-you page, email opens between 47% and 69%, click rate around 12% and four sales. That audience was coaches with a large existing following. Treat Built To Book's real numbers after about two weeks as the baseline and replace the illustrative rates in the MVF map.

## 9. Compliance check before any cold email

This is not legal advice. Steve is in Alberta, so Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) applies to commercial emails to Canadian recipients, and other laws apply to recipients elsewhere. Before any cold email goes out:

- Confirm what consent basis applies (express, implied, or a business-address exemption) and record it per contact.
- Identify Steve and Built To Book clearly, with a real postal address, in every message.
- Include a working unsubscribe link and honor it promptly.
- Keep it targeted business-to-business: a relevant, personalized reason for contacting each owner, not bulk blasts.
- Build the list from public business data (Scrap.io, Apollo, Clay), verify addresses, and remove anyone who has opted out.
- Send from a domain warmed for the purpose, not the main domain.
- Consider a short call with a lawyer or check current CRTC guidance before scaling.

Warm outreach (people who requested a mockup, replied, or asked) is the priority; cold email is a small, careful test on top.
