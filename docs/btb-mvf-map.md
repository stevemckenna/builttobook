# Built To Book: Minimum Viable Funnel (MVF) Map

Source: MVF exercise from a private training video, worked through for Built To Book (2026-09-29). Private reference only. Do not quote it or name the source in any post. "Funnel" is an internal word only. Never use it in client-facing copy (voice rules).

Status key: **Confirmed** = Steve said it or it was verified in a system. **Draft** = Claude's assumption, Steve to correct.

Builds on: `btb-launch-and-content-plan.md`, `btb-offer-doc-draft.md`, `btb-positioning.md`, `btb-homepage-build.md`, `btb-ghl-form-setup.md` (not in this repo yet; the form spec was never saved as a separate doc). Pricing only from `docs/grnded-website-ghl-packages.md`.

The idea: a flow simple enough to fit on a napkin. Every person who enters gets tracked, contacted, given an offer, and then sorted. Build once, use daily. Do not over-build. The bigger "conversion ecosystem" comes later.

---

## 1. The napkin (Draft)

**New lead** → **Lead tracked** → **Contacted** → **Offer made** → **Pitch, punt or persevere**

| Step | What it means for Built To Book |
|---|---|
| New lead | Anyone who engages: a follow, a comment, a DM, a mockup request, a value-trade reply |
| Lead tracked | Added to the GHL CRM as a contact and an opportunity. Nothing lives only in someone's head or in a DM thread |
| Contacted | A DM, email or text goes out (see scripts below) |
| Offer made | Free mockup request, a 15-minute value-trade call, or the offer document (Version A cold, Version B warm) |
| Pitch, punt or persevere | Pitch: they are ready, send Version B and the checkout. Punt: not a fit or not now, close with a reason. Persevere: keep nurturing with email and follow-up |

## 4. CRM pipeline stages (Draft)

1. New lead
2. Contacted
3. Mockup requested
4. Mockup sent
5. Call booked
6. Offer sent (Version B)
7. Won
8. Lost (record the reason)
9. Nurture (persevere)

Custom fields worth capturing (from the homepage form spec): trade, website URL, package interest, budget range, lead volume, urgency, current CRM, source (which post or platform).

## 5. Automations to build (Draft)

1. **New lead alert:** contact created, so Steve is notified (text or email)
2. **Instant reply:** text and email within a minute of a mockup request, asking one question and offering a booking link
3. **Booked call:** confirmation and reminder, then move to "Call booked"
4. **No-reply follow-up:** a short sequence for people who do not answer
5. **Welcome email sequence** after a request
6. **Nurture:** monthly email to "Nurture" contacts

Note: this is the same speed-to-lead system Built To Book sells, so building it for ourselves is also proof and a case-study source.

## 6. Tracking and lead efficiency

**Tracking plan (Confirmed):** form start and completion, contact created, first response, booked call.

**Goal (Confirmed):** five new clients a month.

**Lead efficiency math (Draft, illustrative only, rates are assumptions to replace with real data):**

| Lead-to-client rate | Leads needed for 5 clients | About per day |
|---|---|---|
| 3% | about 167 | 5 to 6 |
| 5% | 100 | 3 to 4 |
| 10% | 50 | 1 to 2 |

Higher-priced packages need fewer clients for the same revenue, so steering interested owners toward Lead System and Local Authority lowers the number of leads required. Measure the real rate from the CRM after the first few weeks and re-run this table.

A lead is anyone who engages, even if still cold. That is why the DM scripts and welcome sequence exist.

## 7. Starter DM scripts (Draft; Steve edits in his voice)

1. **After a follow or like:** "Hey [name], thanks for the follow. Are you running [trade] full time, or on the side?"
2. **Keyword reply (someone comments "call" or "mockup"):** "Thanks [name]. Quick one so I point you the right way: do you already have a website, or starting fresh?"
3. **Value-trade invite:** "I'm talking with trades owners for 15 minutes about how leads come in and where they go cold. No pitch. I'll show you what I'm building at the end. Free this week?"
4. **After a mockup request:** "Got your request. I'll put your concept together from your real work. What's your best service area so I get it right?"
5. **Follow-up if quiet:** "No stress if the timing's off. Want me to send the mockup anyway so you can look when you have a minute?"
6. **Punt with grace:** "Sounds like it's not the right time. If a slow week comes up, I'm here. Anyone you know who's losing calls while on a job?"

Rules: short, plain, no jargon, one question at a time, no fake urgency, and never promise results.

## 8. Lead flow, simplest version (Draft)
