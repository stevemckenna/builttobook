# Built To Book: Golden Interview Questions

Source: Michelle Nedelec (business coach), shared 2026-09-30 after Steve's first coaching call. Private reference. Do not quote it or name the source in any public content.

## What it is for

A short customer interview that gets owners to describe their problem in their own words. Steve uses it to write website and ad copy in the customer's language, and to get owners to put a number on the problem before any pricing conversation.

Michelle's rule of thumb: interview about 8 people. The same words and phrases will repeat, and those become the bold headlines. The story goes underneath.

## The questions

Replace "leadership" in question 1 with the topic being asked about. For trades owners, use something like "getting steady work" or "keeping your calendar full".

1. When it comes to "leadership", what would you say your biggest concern is?
2. Have you tried anything to "fix" this?
3. What did you like about that/those?
4. What didn't you like about that/those?
5. Have you given up trying to find a solution?
6. If you don't fix this issue, what impact will that have on your business?
7. If you don't fix this issue, what impact would it have on you personally?
8. If you did find the ideal solution, what would it look like? (Books, web portal, experiential...)
9. If someone were to offer that solution, how much would you be willing to pay for that?

## How to run it

- Record every call (Steve already does). Pull exact phrases afterward.
- Push for a real number on question 9. If they can't give one, ask again with the math: "You have a problem worth X a year. How much would you put into fixing it?" Vague answers are a signal the owner hasn't made a money decision yet.
- Listen for venting on questions 6 and 7. Those moments are the best copy, especially how the problem hits them personally.
- Interviews cost nothing. They are also a good way to hear how trades owners describe the problem before writing anything.
- Use the exact words on the page. Keep everything in plain English, with no tech or marketing words.
