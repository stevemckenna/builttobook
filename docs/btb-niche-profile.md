## 1. Niche and who we want to be around

- **Confirmed:** Any established local home-service business is viable. Do not exclude any trade: roofing and exteriors, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, remodel, concrete, landscaping, permanent lighting, epoxy garage coatings, granite and quartz countertop fabricators, flooring retailers, and similar. Narrow to specific trades at the campaign level later, not in the brand or general content.
- **Confirmed:** Both owner-operators (1 to 10 crew) and established firms (10+ staff).
- **Draft:** Hands-on owners who take pride in the work and want a site and systems that match it.
- **Draft:** Steve's voice is down to earth (family, beach, sports), not "agency guru." Content should sound like him. Being off-brand attracts worse clients.
- **Content note (Draft):** Visual trades (epoxy, lighting, countertops, flooring) make easy before/after Reels, Shorts and TikTok, so they can be featured often. That is a format choice, not a niche limit. General posts should work for every trade.

## 2. Niche language (Draft)

Use: jobs, quotes, estimates, crew, callbacks, booked out, tire kickers, word of mouth, install, finished project, hiring, good techs.
Avoid: funnel, conversion, lead gen, disruptive, cutting-edge, leverage, AI-powered.

## 3. The three lenses (Confirmed direction, Steve 2026-09-29)

Marketing is not only about sales. Content should speak to three outcomes, not one.

1. **Win better jobs (sales).** More qualified inquiries, fewer tire kickers, a site that matches the work.
2. **Attract better talent (hiring).** A strong brand, website and online reputation make good techs, installers and office staff want to work there. Marketing is a real advantage in recruiting, and it is often overlooked.
3. **Serve customers better while taking work off the owner (CRM and follow-up).** Fast replies, appointment reminders, review requests and status updates improve the customer experience and remove manual work from the owner's plate.

**The reassurance message (Confirmed):** These owners often work 24/7. Content must reassure them that better systems are doing their customers a favor as well as themselves: faster answers, clearer communication, nobody left waiting while the crew is on a job. This is service, not marketing theater. Guilt about "not being reachable" is a real emotion to address.

## 4. Five pain points and what each feels like (Draft, updated for the three lenses)

1. Phone rings, but with tire kickers and wrong-fit jobs. Feels frustrating. (sales)
2. Website looks worse than the work. Feels embarrassing. (sales, talent)
3. Leads and customers go quiet because nobody follows up while the crew is on a job. Feels stretched thin, and guilty about leaving people waiting. (service, owner workload)
4. Hard to find on Google and Maps, few reviews. Feels invisible. (sales)
5. Hard to hire and keep good people; the company looks smaller or less serious online than it is. Feels stuck. (talent)

Also present: past web guys overpromised or locked them in (distrust; shows up as an objection).

## 5. Goals and desires (Draft)

Booked out with better jobs; a steady pipeline; a strong team that stays; customers who feel looked after; evenings and weekends back; an online reputation that matches the in-person one.

## 6. Fears (the pain over a longer horizon) and dream (Draft)

- Fears: a slow season with no pipeline; relying on referrals alone; losing jobs to a competitor with weaker work but a better site; being unable to hire and burning out; letting customers down because they cannot answer every call.
- Dream: a reliable schedule of high-margin jobs, a team they are proud of, customers who rave, and time that is not 24/7.

## 7. Common objections (Draft)

"No time." "Word of mouth is enough." "I got burned before." "Too expensive." "My nephew does the site." "Customers want to talk to me, not a system." (Answer: the system handles the routine so the owner can give real attention where it counts.)

## 8. Emerging trends (Draft)

Google Maps and AI-assisted search; review volume; text-back and fast follow-up; mobile-first sites; Meta lead ads; a tight labor market where good techs choose employers by how they look online.

## 9. Value of the transformation

- **Confirmed:** Varies a lot by trade. Set a range per trade at campaign level.
- Framing by lens: an extra booked job (sales); one good hire who stays (talent); hours per week back and fewer missed customers (service).

## 10. Purchasing power (Draft, Steve to set the bar)

Packages are $297 to $1,997 setup plus $147 to $497 a month (see the package document for current pricing). Filter for established shops with real job values. Minimum bar still to decide.

## 11. Why they haven't solved it, and what they've tried (Draft)

Busy on the tools; nobody owns marketing or follow-up; DIY site builders; agencies that overpromised.
