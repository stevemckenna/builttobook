## 1. Launch (Confirmed)

- **Line in the sand:** Steve is starting promotion now (2026-09-29). He says there are no major blockers he cannot overcome immediately.
- **Reminder, not a stop sign (Draft):** the Visual Identity Guide says trademark and domain clearance comes before launch, and the GHL mockup form is not built yet, so leads need a place to land. Before spending on paid promotion or printing anything, confirm clearance. Organic posting can proceed.
- **Attitude:** A launch is a line in the sand, not a pressure event. Quiet results are fine. Either sales or feedback is a win; feedback tells us what to fix.
- **Goal of the first weeks:** speak with as many trades owners as possible.

### Launch sequence (Draft, roughly 9 to 12 posts)

1. Pre-hype and build in public: the brand reveal, the free mockup process, behind the scenes
2. **Value trade:** a 15-minute call, asking about their leads and follow-up, then showing the offer. Not a bait and switch. If not interested, ask why or who else might be
3. **Power offer:** a post testing the offer ("would you take me up on it?")
4. Core stories: origin, vehicle, case study (once real), internal and external
5. Short-form promo: the October Fast-Action Bonus
6. Then review data and optimize

## 2. Lead efficiency (Confirmed goal, Draft math)

- **Goal (Confirmed):** five new clients a month.
- **Illustrative math from the package document (Draft):** if all five chose Lead System, first-month cash due today would be 5 x $1,294 = $6,470, and recurring monthly revenue would rise by 5 x $297 = $1,485. Re-check prices before using. Mix of packages will change this.
- **Unknowns to track:** conversations held, calls booked from content, mockups requested, close rate. Work backward once real data exists: clients needed / close rate = conversations needed. Do not guess rates.

## 3. Core premise: the big idea (Confirmed)

**Built by an operator.** The website and follow-up system built by someone who has run trades companies. It combines two of the three capabilities (site plus follow-up as one system; operator experience) and is hard to copy. Repeat this premise across all content. The rotating lead pains (missed calls, website worse than the work, not on Google, owner tied to the phone) are angles into this one idea, not replacements for it.

## 4. Core stories (five to ten)

The quality of stories decides the quality of clients. Steve must supply these in his own words. The assistant will not invent them.

- **Origin:** the scaling years at the epoxy and countertop companies (Confirmed setting)
- **Vehicle:** how Steve found the method he now sells

- **Rule:** Post stories in full, then take pieces out of each and reuse them in other posts so the narrative stays consistent. Numbers stay unspecific. Steve approves any figure.

## 5. The seven content types (Draft ideas for Built To Book)

| Type | What it is | Ideas |
|---|---|---|
| You-based | Personal, without family | The office, Calgary, a side quest (not chosen yet), how Steve works |
| Authority | "How I" stories that show you know the work | How Steve handled follow-up when the phones were slammed; how a site and a follow-up system work together |
| Case studies | Any quick win, told as a story | Free mockup reveals for real prospects, with permission; none exist yet |
| Offers | Direct and indirect | Direct: request a free mockup, October bonus. Indirect: "who wants a mockup of their site?" |
| Journey identification | "If you're at X, here's what to focus on" | Just starting a company: get found first. Busy crew: answer every call. Hiring: look like a place good techs want to work |
| Hot takes and enemies | Practices, never named companies | Template sites hide great work; agencies that lock you in; chasing every lead by hand; agencies that do not understand trades |
| Borrowed authority | Building on or reacting to a known person's take | Only with a real, respectful angle. Never imply a relationship or endorsement that does not exist |

## 6. Monthly theme and goodwill cycle (Draft)

**October theme:** Built by an operator. The October Fast-Action Bonus runs across all four weeks (first five sign-ups only; keep the remaining-spots count accurate).

| Week | Focus | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (Sep 29 to 30) | Pre-hype | Brand reveal, what is being built, value-trade call invitation |
| 1 (Oct 1 to 7) | Look the part | Website that matches the work; free mockup |
| 2 (Oct 8 to 14) | Answer fast | Missed-call text-back, follow-up, no lead left waiting |
| 3 (Oct 15 to 21) | Get found | Google profile, reviews, local pages |
| 4 (Oct 22 to 31) | Grow and keep | Past customers, hiring (content only; the hiring add-on is not scoped or priced), monthly review |

Note from the training: deep, unglamorous themes attract better clients, and high-volume topics attract more but lower-quality ones. Track which weeks bring which kind of client.

## 7. Cadence (Confirmed: daily short video plus a few text posts)

- **Video:** one short video a day, filmed mainly in Steve's office (Confirmed), repurposed to Facebook Reels, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok. AI-generated jobsite imagery is atmosphere only, never proof.
- **Text and image posts:** a few a week on Facebook and Instagram.
- **Mix (Draft):** mostly conversion content, with some reach content so there are people to talk to.

### Weekly template (Draft)

- **Mon:** core story (full or a piece of it)
- **Tue:** authority piece ("how I")
- **Wed:** indirect call to action (mockup)
- **Thu:** hot take (an enemy practice)
- **Fri:** case study or mockup reveal (only real ones, with permission)
- **Sat and Sun:** you-based or journey identification
- **Every day:** follow up by direct message with anyone who engages; log leads and calls booked

Weekly rhythm from the training: write three core stories, make one short piece from each, publish one authority piece, use two soft calls to action, track lead efficiency, follow up with everyone who engages.

## 8. First draft posts (Draft; Steve edits in his voice)

**Value trade (Facebook and Instagram text):**
"I'm building something for trades owners and I want honest feedback before I go further. I've run trade businesses, and I know how it goes: great work, a website that doesn't show it, and a phone that rings when you're on a job. I'm looking for [number] owners for a 15-minute call. I'll ask how leads come in and where they go cold, then show you what I'm building. If it's not for you, I'd still like to know why. Comment 'call' or message me."

**Power offer:**
"If I built your website from your real work, showed it to you before you paid a cent, and set it up so every missed call got a text back within a minute, would you take me up on it? Be honest. What would make you say no?"

**October bonus (only if the count is accurate):**
"First five trades businesses to sign up in October get $100 in text and call credits, plus a Google review QR plaque for the counter or a QR decal for the work truck. Five spots. [X] left."

**Short video hook frameworks (Steve fills in real details):**
- "The best lead doesn't call the best contractor. It calls whoever answers first. Here's how I'd fix that."
- "Your work is better than your website. Here's what a customer sees in the first three seconds."
- "Three things I'd set up before spending a dollar on ads."

Voice reminder: plain talk, no jargon (no funnel, leverage, AI-powered). Every post ends with one clear next step: request a free mockup.
